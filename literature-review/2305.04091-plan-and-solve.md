# Plan-and-Solve Prompting

- **Citation**: Wang et al., ACL 2023 — [arXiv:2305.04091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04091)
- **Cited in**: [design/architecture/01-planning.md](../design/architecture/01-planning.md) §2

## Thesis

Explicitly instructing a model to "devise a plan, then carry it out" (rather than just "think step by step") consistently outperforms Zero-shot Chain-of-Thought across datasets.

## Key findings

- Vanilla CoT error breakdown: 12% step-missing errors, 27% semantic-misunderstanding errors.
- Plan-and-Solve's explicit plan-then-execute framing targets the step-missing failure mode directly.

## What we took from it

**Adopted.** Cheap to implement, well-evidenced, and directly on-target for this project's Gru-plans/minion-executes architecture — this is essentially the two-phase structure the whole project is built around, now with direct supporting evidence for the plan-then-execute framing itself (independent of who executes).

## Caveats

None flagged — this is one of the more directly-adopted, low-controversy findings in the planning research pass.
