# MAMM-Refine

- **Citation**: [arXiv:2503.15272](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.15272)
- **Cited in**: [design/architecture/02-gru-minion-protocol.md](../design/architecture/02-gru-minion-protocol.md) §4

## Thesis

Proposes multi-agent collaboration specifically to *improve* faithfulness by separating drafting, evaluation, and synthesis into distinct roles, rather than one agent doing all three in a single pass.

## Key findings

- Part of a broader literature convergence: "when agents collaborate, hallucinations no longer remain local; they can propagate across agent boundaries and trigger operational failures."
- Splitting drafting/evaluation/synthesis into distinct roles is proposed (not just asserted) as a mitigation for single-pass summarize-and-handoff being a faithfulness weak point.

## What we took from it

Implies that a minion doing single-pass "explore then summarize for Gru" is a known-weak pattern worth architecting around — e.g. by not treating a minion's summary as ground truth, or by structurally separating a minion's exploration from its final report.

## Caveats

**Real, but doesn't answer this project's specific question.** No paper was found that directly quantifies information loss in this project's specific setup — a *cheap* sub-agent summarizing for an *expensive* orchestrator. The literature covers general multi-agent hallucination and strong-summarizes-for-strong, but not this weak-summarizes-for-strong scenario. Flagged as the weakest-evidenced section of that research pass, not papered over.
