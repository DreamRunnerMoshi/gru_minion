# AI Safety via Debate

- **Citation**: Irving, Christiano, Amodei, 2018 — [arXiv:1805.00899](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00899)
- **Cited in**: [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md) §Debate escalation

## Thesis

A bounded/weak verifier, judging a structured *interactive* debate between two adversarial agents, can correctly adjudicate a much larger class of claims than the same verifier could judge by direct inspection alone.

## Key findings

- Direct judgment by a bounded verifier corresponds roughly to **NP** (can check a certificate, can't necessarily find one). Structured interactive debate judged by the same bounded verifier can in principle reach correct answers for questions in **PSPACE** (⊋ NP under standard assumptions) — the same mechanism as the IP=PSPACE theorem: interaction, not a static proof, is what lets a weak judge correctly adjudicate a much larger claim class.
- Empirically demonstrated on a toy MNIST task (a sparse judge sees only a few revealed pixels; two debaters who see the full image argue for true vs. false labels): the honest debater wins **~88.9%** of the time at 6 revealed pixels, well above the judge's unaided accuracy of **59.4%**. Debate recovers far more correct-answer accuracy from a weak judge than direct evaluation of the same bounded evidence.

## What we took from it

**Core mechanism behind this project's debate-escalation step.** When Gru (a bounded verifier relative to the full context two minions might have) needs to adjudicate a disputed judgment call — e.g. two minions disagreeing about whether a fix is complete — structured debate between them, judged by Gru, is theoretically motivated to recover more correct judgments than Gru inspecting the raw context directly, especially when Gru can't feasibly review everything both minions saw firsthand.

## Caveats

**Acknowledged failure mode directly maps onto this project's spec-authorship gap**: the *obfuscated arguments problem*. A dishonest debater can construct an argument that is long, plausible, and contains a fatal flaw structured so that finding it is computationally intractable within the debate's turn budget — breaking the "lying is harder than refuting a lie" equilibrium the whole approach depends on. Same phenomenon as Goodhart's Law / reward hacking, in argument form instead of static-spec form. See [How to Avoid Debate](./2607.03561-doubly-efficient-interactive-proofs.md) for a 2026 attempt at a successor that avoids this specific failure mode.
