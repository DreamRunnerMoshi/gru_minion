# ReWOO: Decoupling Reasoning from Observations for Efficient Augmented Language Models

- **Citation**: [arXiv:2305.18323](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18323)
- **Cited in**: [design/architecture/02-gru-minion-protocol.md](../design/architecture/02-gru-minion-protocol.md) §1, §5.4

## Thesis

Decouples reasoning from observations entirely: plan *all* tool/delegate calls upfront in one pass, fire them (in parallel), then reason once over all results — instead of ReAct's interleave-one-call-at-a-time approach. A genuinely different protocol fork, not a minor variant of ReAct.

## Key findings

- Batching all sub-calls upfront and firing them in parallel is cheaper and faster than interleaved single-call rounds, at the cost of being less adaptive to what earlier results reveal (later calls can't be informed by earlier findings).

## What we took from it

Names the real design fork for how Gru delegates research sub-calls to minions: interleaved (ReAct-style — delegate, observe, delegate again; more adaptive, more round-trips) vs. batched (ReWOO-style — plan all research calls upfront, fire in parallel; cheaper/faster, less adaptive). This project's current lean is a hybrid (batch the obvious calls upfront, allow at most one follow-up round) — but this is this project's own synthesis, not something the literature proves optimal.

## Caveats

**Unresolved fork, not resolved by evidence** — no paper directly compares interleaved vs. batched delegation for this project's specific planning-with-delegated-research use case. The hybrid recommendation in the source doc is a reasonable synthesis, not a cited result.
