# SearchSwarm (delegation-frequency study)

- **Citation**: [arXiv:2606.09730](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09730)
- **Cited in**: [design/architecture/02-gru-minion-protocol.md](../design/architecture/02-gru-minion-protocol.md) §3, §Gaps

## Thesis

Reportedly studies how often a delegating orchestrator chooses to hand work to sub-agents vs. doing it inline.

## Key findings

- Cited figure: a delegating orchestrator's `call_sub_agent` action reportedly accounts for **over 70%** of its tool invocations on some benchmarks — i.e. learned policies favor delegating information-gathering over doing it inline.

## What we took from it

Weak supporting evidence for a general heuristic already assumed in this project's design: delegate when marginal quality gain exceeds marginal cost; don't delegate work cheap enough to do inline. Not used as a hard number anywhere in the design — no paper was found with an actual quantified delegation-frequency-vs-quality-vs-cost curve, which remains a flagged gap.

## Caveats

**Not independently verified.** The 70% figure is sourced from a search snippet — the PDF didn't parse for direct verification during the research pass that cited it. Treat as directional, not confirmed, and re-verify against the paper's own text before relying on it for any design decision.
